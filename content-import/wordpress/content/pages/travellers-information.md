# Travellers Information

Source: https://lotuspeak.org/travellers-information/

## Travel Information for Visitors to Bhutan

Welcome to the Land of the Thunder Dragon!  
This guide provides essential travel information to help you plan a safe, comfortable, and memorable trip to Bhutan. While not a complete list, these tips cover the most important aspects of traveling in the country.

### Travel & Medical Insurance

The **Royal Insurance Corporation of Bhutan (RICB)** offers a dedicated travel and medical insurance plan for visitors. You can arrange coverage through your Bhutanese tour operator, international partner, or directly via [www.ricb.com.bt](https://www.ricb.com.bt/).

We highly recommend securing travel insurance before your trip to ensure peace of mind during your journey.

### Currency & Money

Bhutan’s currency is the **Ngultrum (Nu.)**, which is pegged to the Indian Rupee (INR). Indian Rupees are widely accepted throughout the country.

Most hotels, restaurants, and handicraft shops offer **POS (Point of Sale)** services, allowing you to pay conveniently by **credit or debit card**.

### Banking Services

Bhutan’s banking network is well-developed, with several institutions offering reliable services, including:

* Bank of Bhutan Limited (BoB)
* Bhutan National Bank (BNB)
* Druk PNB Bank
* Tashi Bank

You can easily withdraw or exchange **traveller’s cheques**, and most banks also provide **online and internet banking** facilities.

### Electricity

Electricity in Bhutan runs on **220/240 volts** with **round two-pin or three-pin outlets**.  
It’s advisable to bring a **universal adapter** or **flat-to-round pin converter** for your devices.

Bhutan is proud to be a **carbon-neutral nation**, powered entirely by clean, renewable **hydropower energy**.

### Photography

Bhutan offers breathtaking photography opportunities — from majestic mountain ranges to vibrant cultural scenes.

Please check with your guide **before taking photos inside Dzongs, temples, monasteries, or religious sites**, as photography may be restricted in some areas.  
Feel free to capture the **landscapes, rural life, architecture**, and **Bhutanese festivals** from the outside.

### Shopping

Bhutan is a treasure trove of **handcrafted art and culture**. Popular souvenirs include:

* Hand-woven textiles (silk & raw silk)
* Carved wooden masks
* Bamboo and cane baskets
* Wooden bowls (*Dapas*)
* Handmade paper products
* Silver crafts and Thangka paintings
* Colorful Bhutanese postage stamps

Please note: **Buying or selling antiques is strictly prohibited** in Bhutan.

### Gratuity (Tipping)

Tipping is not mandatory but is considered a thoughtful gesture in Bhutan.  
Visitors often offer gratuities to their **guide and driver** at the end of a trip — usually placed in an envelope.

For treks, it’s customary to also tip the **cook, assistant cook, and horsemen** as a sign of appreciation for their service.

### Communication

Bhutan has a well-established telecommunications network.  
Most hotels, cafés, and restaurants offer **Wi-Fi internet access**.  
Mobile network coverage is strong nationwide, and **international roaming** is available for most operators.

### Clothing & Etiquette

Bhutan’s weather varies by region and altitude, so **pack for mixed conditions**.

When visiting religious sites such as **Dzongs, temples, and monasteries**, please:

* Dress modestly (long pants and long-sleeved shirts)
* Remove hats and caps before entering
* Show respect in areas where the **national flag** is raised

A respectful attitude and modest dress are highly appreciated by the Bhutanese people.

### Measures, Weight & Time

Bhutan follows the **metric system**, and local time is **GMT +6 hours**.

### Health Precautions

Consult your doctor before traveling to Bhutan for recommended vaccinations such as **Tetanus, Typhoid,** and **Hepatitis A**.

Carry any prescribed medications and a small first-aid kit for your journey.

### Safety & General Precautions

Bhutan is one of the **safest countries in the world**, but it’s still wise to take basic precautions:

* Keep valuables like passports, wallets, and cameras secure.
* Avoid leaving belongings unattended or in vehicles.
* Drink only **boiled or bottled water**; avoid ice made from tap water.

Please note that Bhutan has strict laws regarding **tobacco and drugs**.  
Visitors must comply with the **Tobacco Control Act**, which limits the amount of tobacco products allowed for personal use.

### A Final Word

Bhutan’s pristine environment, rich culture, and warm hospitality make it one of the most unique destinations in the world.  
By respecting local customs and environmental values, you contribute to preserving the spirit of Bhutan for generations to come.
