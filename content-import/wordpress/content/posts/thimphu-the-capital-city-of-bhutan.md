# Thimphu, The capital city of bhutan

Source: https://lotuspeak.org/thimphu-the-capital-city-of-bhutan/

**Thimphu** is the capital and largest city of Bhutan, located in the Thimphu Valley along the Wang Chhu River at an elevation of approximately 2,320 meters (7,656 feet). With a population of around 115,000–120,000, it is the political, economic, and cultural center of the country.

Historically a small village, Thimphu became the capital in the 1960s under King Jigme Dorji Wangchuck as Bhutan began modernizing. The city has since grown rapidly while maintaining a mix of traditional Bhutanese architecture and modern infrastructure.

## **Buddha Dordenma**

A monumental 169-foot-tall statue of Buddha, one of the largest in the world, located atop a hill offering panoramic views of the Thimphu Valley. The site houses over 100,000 smaller Buddha statues, creating a serene and spiritual atmosphere.

![buddha-point](https://lotuspeak.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/buddha-point-300x106.webp)

## Here are the key details about this site:

* **Location:** Perched atop a hill in the Kuenselphodrang Nature Park, it offers panoramic, scenic views of the southern entrance to the Thimphu Valley.
* **Significance:** Completed in September 2015 to celebrate the 60th anniversary of the fourth King, Jigme Singye Wangchuck, it was built to fulfill an ancient prophecy dating back to the 8th century, believed to bring peace and happiness to the world.
* **Structure:** The statue is made of bronze and gilded in gold. Inside the statue are 125,000 smaller Buddha statues—100,000 that are 8 inches tall and 25,000 that are 12 inches tall.
* **Activities:** It is a major tourist attraction and a place of pilgrimage. Visitors can walk around the site, enjoy the views, and hike on surrounding trails, including a trail that leads to Changangkha.
* **Lighting:** The statue is illuminated at night, providing a spectacular view from the city.

## **Dochula Pass**

Dochula Pass is one of Bhutan’s most scenic and culturally significant mountain passes, located at an elevation of **3,100 meters (10,171 feet)** on the road between the capital city, Thimphu, and the historical town of Punakha. It is famous for its panoramic views of the Himalayan range and its 108 memorial chortens (stupas).

![](https://lotuspeak.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/dochhula-1024x683.jpg)

## Key Features of Dochula Pass

* 1**08 Druk Wangyal Chortens:** Built in 2004, these 108 memorial chortens (stupas) were commissioned by the eldest Queen Mother, Ashi Dorji Wangmo Wangchuk, to honor Bhutanese soldiers who died in a 2003 military operation. The chortens are arranged in three layers around a central temple.
* **Panoramic Himalayan Views:** On clear days (best from October to February), the pass provides stunning 360-degree views of the snow-capped Eastern Himalayas, including **Mount Gangkar Puensum (7,158m)**, which is known as the highest unclimbed peak in the world.
* **Druk Wangyal Lhakhang (Temple):** Built in 2008, this temple commemorates 100 years of monarchy and features unique murals that blend traditional stories with modern Bhutanese history, such as monks with laptops or the fourth king’s military actions.
* **Druk Wangyel Tshechu:** Held annually on **December 13th**, this unique festival is performed by the Royal Bhutan Army rather than monks, featuring special mask dances to celebrate the 2003 victory.
* **Surrounding Nature:** The area is adorned with colorful prayer flags and surrounded by rhododendron forests, making it a popular spot for photography and nature walks.

## Visitor Information

* **Best Time to Visit:** The best time for clear views is **October to February** (autumn and winter). Spring (March to May) is ideal for seeing the rhododendrons in bloom.
* **Location & Access:** It is about 22–30 km from Thimphu and serves as a, roughly, 45-minute to 1.5-hour drive on the way to Punakha.
* **Tips:** It is usually cold and windy; warm clothing is recommended. The site can be very crowded with tourists and local families on weekends or high season.

The pass also features a cafeteria, offering visitors a place to warm up with hot coffee or tea while enjoying the view.

## **Memorial Chorten**

National Memorial Chorten in Thimphu is one of Bhutan’s most iconic religious landmarks, often called “the most visible religious structure in the city”. Built in 1974, it serves as a memorial to the third King of Bhutan, **Jigme Dorji Wangchuck** (the “Father of Modern Bhutan”), and as a monument dedicated to world peace

![memorial-chorten](https://lotuspeak.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/memorial-chorten-300x171.webp)

## Key Highlights

* **Architecture:** It is a Tibetan-style *Jangchup Chorten* featuring a distinct vase-like shape rather than a standard dome. It is topped with a golden spire that gleams in the sunlight.
* **Interior:** The three-story interior is filled with intricate paintings and statues of wrathful deities and mandalas following the Nyingma tradition of Tibetan Buddhism.
* **Daily Life:** From dawn until dusk, you can witness elderly Bhutanese and monks circumambulating (walking around) the stupa in a clockwise direction while spinning large red prayer wheels and chanting mantras.

## Visitor Information

* **Location:** Doeboom Lam, near the main roundabout in southern-central Thimphu.
* **Entry Fee:** A fee for international tourists is **USD 15** for adults and **USD 8** for those under 18. (Note: Fees are subject to change based on current Sustainable Development Fee policies).
* **Hours:** Generally open from **6:00 AM to 6:00 PM**.
* **Note:** Photography is strictly prohibited inside the temple, though you may take photos of the exterior and gardens.

## **Simtokha Dzong**

**Simtokha Dzong**, officially known as ***Sangak Zabdhon Phodrang*** (“Palace of the Profound Meaning of Secret Mantras”), is the first fortress built by **Zhabdrung Ngawang Namgyal** in 1629. It is the oldest dzong in Bhutan to survive in its original complete form and served as the model for all subsequent castle-monasteries in the kingdom.

![simtokha](https://lotuspeak.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/simtokha-300x200.webp)

## **Historical Significance**

* **The First Dzong:** While older structures existed, this was the first to combine **monastic and administrative** functions under one roof.
* **Strategic Location:** It sits on a prominent ridge 5km south of Thimphu, at the boundary of three major western regions: Thimphu (*Wang*), Paro (*Pa*), and Wangdue Phodrang (*Sha*).
* **The Demon Legend:** The name “Simtokha” is derived from *sinmo* (demoness) and *do* (stone). Legend says Zhabdrung built the dzong over a rock where he had imprisoned a demoness that was terrorizing travelers.

## **Key Features to See**

* **Slate Carvings:** The exterior of the central tower (*utse*) is lined with over **300 finely worked slate carvings** from the 17th century depicting various Buddhist saints and philosophers.
* **Ancient Murals:** The main chapel houses some of the oldest mural paintings in Bhutan, including a unique **cosmic mandala** on the ceiling of the assembly hall.
* **Main Relics:** The primary inner sanctum features a large statue of **Sakyamuni Buddha** (Buddha of Compassion) flanked by eight Bodhisattvas.
* **Guardian Chapel:** The eastern *goenkhang* (protector chapel) contains ancient weapons and preserved **tiger tails** hanging from pillars, dedicated to Bhutan’s protector deities, Mahakala and Pelden Lhamo

## **Visitor Information (2026)**

* **Entry Fee:** Starting **January 1, 2026**, foreign visitors are charged an entry fee of **Nu 500** (approx. USD 6). Children aged 6–17 receive a 50% concession, and children under 5 are free.
* **Hours:** Open daily from **9:00 AM to 5:00 PM**. On government holidays, it may open later at 10:00 AM.
* **Dress Code:** Visitors must wear formal clothing that covers arms and legs to enter.
* **Location:** Semtokha Lam, about a 15-20 minute drive south of Thimphu city center.

## **Motithang Takin Preserve**

**Motithang Takin Preserve**, this is the best place to see Bhutan’s national animal—the **Takin** (*Budorcas taxicolor*). It’s less of a “zoo” and more of a large, forested enclosure where these unique creatures roam freely.

![zoo](https://lotuspeak.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/zoo-300x148.webp)

**The “Cow-Goat” Legend**  
Local legend says the Takin was created in the 15th century by the “Divine Madman,” **Lama Drukpa Kunley**. After eating a whole cow and a whole goat for lunch, he supposedly stuck the goat’s head onto the cow’s skeleton and commanded it to walk. Science describes it as a “bovid” related to the muskox, but the legend is much more fun.

## **Quick Visitor Facts**

* **Entry Fee:** According to The Bhutanese, international visitors are charged **Nu 500** (approx. USD 6).
* **Best Time to Visit:** Arrive in the **early morning** (around 9:00 AM) when the Takins are most active and near the fences for feeding. They tend to retreat into the shade of the trees during the afternoon heat.
* **Location:** Located in the Motithang district, a short drive uphill from Thimphu city center.
* **Other Wildlife:** You can also spot **Sambar deer** and **Barking deer** within the preserve.

The preserve is located right next to the start of several popular hiking trails. After seeing the Takins, you can take a short walk up to the **Sangaygang Viewpoint** for one of the best overhead views of the Thimphu valley.
