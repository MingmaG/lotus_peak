# Punakha: The Dzong

Source: https://lotuspeak.org/punakha-the-dzong/

Punakha Dzong, also known as **Pungthang Dewa Chhenbi Phodrang** (“The Palace of Great Happiness”), is widely considered the most beautiful and historically significant fortress in Bhutan.

## Significance

* **Political & Administrative History**: Built in 1637 by Zhabdrung Ngawang Namgyal, it served as the capital of Bhutan and seat of government until 1955. It was the coronation site for the first King of Bhutan in 1907 and continues to be where all Bhutanese monarchs are crowned.

* **Religious Importance**: It is the second oldest and second largest dzong in the country. It serves as the winter residence of the **Je Khenpo** (Chief Abbot) and the Central Monastic Body, who move here from Thimphu every winter.
* **Sacred Relics**: The fortress houses the preserved remains of the Zhabdrung and the sacred **Rangjung Kharsapani**, a self-created image of Avalokiteswara.
* **Architectural Marvel**: Situated at the confluence of the **Pho Chhu** (Male) and **Mo Chhu** (Female) rivers, the dzong features a unique three-courtyard design rather than the standard two

### 2026 Travel Information

* **Entry Fees**:  
  + **Adults**: Nu 500.
  + **Children (6–17 years)**: Nu 250.
  + **Children (under 6)**: Free.
* **Visiting Hours**:  
  + **June to Mid-November**: 9:00 AM – 5:00 PM.
  + **Mid-November to May**: 11:00 AM – 1:00 PM and 3:00 PM – 5:00 PM.
* **Best Time to Visit**:  
  + **Spring (March–May)**: Features the blooming of lilac **jacaranda flowers** around the whitewashed walls.
  + **Punakha Tshechu**: A major festival held in February or March featuring a dramatic re-enactment of the 1639 victory over Tibetan invaders. For 2026, the festival is scheduled for **February 27 to March 1**.
* **Access & Tips**:  
  + The dzong is reached via a traditional wooden cantilever bridge (**Bazam**) crossing the Mo Chhu river.
  + Photography is permitted in the courtyards but strictly prohibited inside the temples and shrines.
  + **Dress Code**: Visitors must wear modest attire (long sleeves and pants/skirts) and remove shoes before entering temple rooms
