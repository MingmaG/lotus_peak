# Splendid view of trongsa dzong

Source: https://lotuspeak.org/splendid-view-of-trongsa-dzong/

Trongsa Dzong, often referred to as the **Chhoekhor Raptentse Dzong**, is considered one of the most spectacular, Largest, and historically significant fortresses in Bhutan. It is famously perched on a steep ridge, offering commanding, panoramic views of the Mangde Chhu river valley and surrounding mountains.

## Significance

* **Royal Ancestry**: It is the ancestral seat of the **Wangchuck Dynasty**. By tradition, the Crown Prince of Bhutan must serve as the **Trongsa Penlop** (Governor) before ascending the throne.
* **Historical Command**: Built in 1644 on a steep ridge, its strategic location allowed it to control all trade between **Eastern and Western Bhutan** for centuries.
* **Religious Hub**: It houses the second-largest monastic body in the country, with approximately 200 monks, and contains 25 temples and a notable printing house for religious texts.
* **Architecture**: Recognized as an architectural marvel, it features a labyrinth of buildings, courtyards, and corridors that follow the natural contours of the mountain.

## 2026 Travel Information

Effective **January 1, 2026**, new monument management guidelines and fees are in place.

* **Dress Code**: Formal dress is required for entry; shoulders and knees must be covered, and photography is restricted in certain sacred areas

* **Visiting Hours**: 9:00 AM – 5:00 PM daily. Note that the Dzong remains closed to tourists outside these hours.

* **Entry Fees (New for 2026)**:

* **Adults (18+ years)**: Nu 500.

* **Children (6–17 years)**: Nu 250.

* **Children (under 6)**: Free.

> All fees are subjective to change in near future

## **Best Time to Visit**:

* **Festival**: The annual 5-day **Trongsa Tsechu** (mask dance festival) occurs in December or January and culminates in the unveiling of a giant sacred thangka.

* **Weather**: March to May and September to November offer clear skies and moderate temperatures.

* **Access**: Located in central Bhutan, it is roughly a 7-hour drive from Thimphu. Active hikers can approach via the **Mangdue Foot Trail** for a traditional entry through the western gate.
